# Field accuracy test: photo measurement vs. tape measure

Goal: find out how close GroundsOps photo measurements are to a tape measure on real jobs,
before a customer relies on them to order materials. Do it on **5 to 10 jobs**. The app
collects the results under **AI quality → Field accuracy**.

A printable version with a record sheet is in [`field-accuracy-test.pdf`](field-accuracy-test.pdf).

## What you need

- A 100 ft tape measure (or a measuring wheel for long edges).
- Two sheets of plain white letter paper (8.5 × 11 in), or a patio slab of known size.
- A few small stones to hold the paper flat if it is windy.
- Your phone with GroundsOps open.

## On each job (about 15 minutes)

1. **Place the paper.** Lay one sheet flat on the ground inside the work area, about a third
   of the way in from the side nearest you. It must be flat, uncreased and fully visible.
   On grass, press it down or weigh the corners with stones. White paper on dark soil or
   grass is easiest for the app to find.
2. **Take the photo.** Stand 10 to 20 ft back at chest height with the whole area in frame.
   Use the normal 1× camera, not the 0.5× ultra-wide lens, which bends straight lines.
   On the screen the paper should look at least as big as a fingernail.
3. **Measure in the app.** Upload the photo in Design studio, paint exactly the area you
   will tape-measure, choose **Known object**, pick *Letter paper* and tap the sheet. Check
   the yellow corners sit on the paper's corners (drag them if not). Note the area.
4. **Tape-measure the same area.** Use the same edges you painted.
   - Rectangle: length × width.
   - Odd shapes: split into rectangles and triangles (triangle = ½ × base × height) and add
     them up. Write the pieces in the notes.
   - Curved beds: split the curve into short straight sections, or measure the edge with a
     wheel and the widest and narrowest widths, and note it as approximate.
5. **Record it.** Generate the concept, open **Tape check** under it, and enter the tape
   area (and edge length if you measured it). The app shows how far off it was.

Optional, on one or two jobs: also draw a **Line** over the paper's long edge (11 in) on the
same photo, to compare the old line method with the known-object method.

## Reading the results

| Typical error | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| 5% or less | Good for ordering. Normal sod and paver waste allowances (5 to 10%) cover it. |
| 5 to 10% | Fine for quoting; double-check before ordering expensive materials. |
| Over 10% | Look at that photo: paper too small or far away, ultra-wide lens, sloped ground, or the painted area did not match the taped area. |

Photo measurements assume the ground is roughly flat. Slopes, mounds and steps make the
area larger than it looks from above, so tape those jobs separately.
